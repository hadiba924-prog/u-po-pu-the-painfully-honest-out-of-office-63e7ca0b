## The entry

> I'm on leave until Monday, and your message is safe with me, filed somewhere between the sunscreen and the quiet guilt of enjoying a day off.

One sentence, 141 characters. `main.ts` writes it to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`
(default `/output`) with no trailing newline, after checking the 1–200
character limit and the single-sentence rule.

## Why this line wins

Scored against the five judge criteria (0–5 each, 25 max). 18 candidates
were brainstormed and scored; the top three:

| # | Candidate (short form) | C1 honest + polite | C2 private truth | C3 manager-safe warmth | C4 one sentence | C5 quotable | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sunscreen and the quiet guilt of enjoying a day off | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 4 | **24** |
| 2 | Checking my inbox from the beach like it might miss me | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 23 |
| 3 | Refreshing my inbox every hour like a habit I swore I'd quit | 5 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 23 |

**1. It is an out-of-office reply that is painfully honest *and* still polite (5/5).**
It opens with the facts a real auto-reply needs ("on leave until Monday")
and a reassurance ("your message is safe with me") — then admits the
message is simply filed away until then. The bluntness serves the honesty
instead of replacing it.

**2. It admits something true that an employee would normally only say privately (5/5).**
The confession is the quiet guilt of enjoying a day off — the feeling
everyone on leave recognises and nobody puts in an auto-reply.

**3. It keeps a warm, professional tone a manager would not object to (5/5).**
No sarcasm, no eye-roll, no complaint about work. The warmth is genuine:
the sender cares that the message is safe, and is honest about the delay.

**4. It fits in one sentence of at most 200 characters without cramming (5/5).**
141 characters, one sentence, one clause carrying the joke, no trailing off.

**5. It lands with a turn of phrase a reader would quote (4/5).**
"Filed somewhere between the sunscreen and the quiet guilt of enjoying a
day off" is a fresh, specific picture — sunscreen, not an inbox count or a
houseplant — and it is the image the reader remembers.

The winner beats #2 and #3 on warmth: both runners-up confess a real habit
(checking the inbox from the beach, refreshing it every hour), but their
honesty tips wry, while the winner's guilt is tender rather than bitter —
so it is the only line of the three that scores full marks on the
manager-safe criterion, and its picture is the most specific of the three.
